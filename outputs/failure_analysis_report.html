The main finding is an identity problem. Fifteen records display replacements under “Sub On, Play On.” Do not automatically attach the displayed counter to the substitute or interpret these as 15 failed pre-match substitute picks.

What the results support

The original report remains 119 hits / 150 assessed groups (79.3%), including 31 misses: 13 zeros and 18 ones. These are report classifications, not independently confirmed settlement results or a corrected full-match player dataset. External enrichment below goes beyond the original report-only phase.

Among six differences between the displayed counter and the displayed player's externally reported shots, five checks show that the counter instead matches the original player: Marmoush → Maddison (1 versus 0), Giroud → Ueda (0 versus 2), Doué → Kvaratskhelia (1 versus 2), Díaz → Bischof (1 versus 0), and Zinckernagel → Dithejane (0 versus 1). This supports a counter-attribution hypothesis, not a blanket correction. Pep Biel → De La Torre still needs original-player reconciliation. Antony and Bouanga also lack sufficiently verified original totals.

Ueda and Kvaratskhelia each have two personal shots in the candidate match. Calling those displayed players full-match 2+ failures is therefore unsafe. It does not automatically establish that the original betting selections won. Jackson and Abraham both have zero, which also shows that agreement alone cannot identify whose counter was displayed.

Bet365's Sub On, Play On rules distinguish unresolved selections that transfer from already determined outcomes. Consequently, a replacement label can depend on what happened during the match. The earlier 0/15 replacement hit rate must not be used as a causal finding or a pre-match predictor. “No replacement note” also does not establish a start or a stable role.

Failure categories to investigate

These categories are mutually exclusive triage groups, not proven causes. All 31 records are assigned below.

  • Replacement identity / counter reconciliation: 15
  • Low personal volume despite 80+ minutes: 11
  • Individual context incomplete: 2
  • Shorter starting appearance: 1
  • Selected player had a substitute appearance: 2

Ingvartsen and Gauld were substitutes despite having no replacement note. Their limited exposure is relevant to a minutes hypothesis. Several other players had at least 80 provider minutes yet still registered zero or one shot, so insufficient playing time cannot explain all failures. Coburn's shorter starting appearance is a separate case; the evidence does not establish why he was removed.

Team context and personal involvement also differ: Judd took one of San Jose's 11 shots, while Navarro took one of Colorado's nine. Budimir scored his sole attempt, a penalty. João Pedro and Messi contributed assists while finishing on one shot. These examples support investigating player shot share and role; goals, assists and the final score do not by themselves explain repeated shooting.

Evidence coverage and limits

28 candidate fixtures cover the 31 records. Personal shot totals were located for 25/31 displayed players; 6 differ from the PDF counter. Original-player minutes and personal totals were separately checked for twelve replacement records. Other original-player fields remain unverified.

All dates are candidate links inferred from pairing, score and player evidence. The ticket history does not expose match dates, so these are not definitive ticket-to-match joins. MLS local dates can differ from UTC dates. Provider minutes and substitution-event minutes use different conventions; both are retained where relevant. Some statistics were available only through indexed FBref search extracts because direct pages blocked access. Those figures need direct-provider confirmation before final adjudication.

Ticket statuses are provenance only: a lost multi-selection ticket does not prove that a particular leg lost, and a cash-out does not establish when a displayed statistic was captured. No corrected overall hit rate is calculated because the 119 reported hits have not received the same external audit.

Settlement audit: 14 reported misses occur only on cashed-out tickets among their assessed occurrences. Ryan Gauld’s zero-shot MISS is attached to a ticket marked Won. That is an unresolved inconsistency requiring the original leg settlement record; the numeric PDF classification must not be equated with a settled losing bet.

Hypotheses and the evidence needed next

  1. Counter attribution: reconcile all 15 original/replacement pairs against dated ticket screenshots or exports, separate both players' own shots, and establish the qualifying settlement counter and cash-out timing.
  2. Minutes: compare original selected players' expected and actual minutes in both hits and misses. A replacement's short appearance cannot explain why the original player fell short.
  3. Personal shot share: obtain team shots while the player was on the pitch, the player's own attempts and comparable prior starts. Separate low team production from a small personal share.
  4. Role and game events: record actual position, lineup, score at substitutions and red-card timing. Compare similar successful selections before treating these as recurring causal patterns.
  5. Repeat-player analysis: rebuild histories around original selected identities. Rio's two displayed failures refer to Barcola and Gakpo; they are not necessarily two separate original Ngumoha selections.

Conclusion for this stage: there are usable failure cases and specific hypotheses, but no defensible single “reason for failure” for every row yet. Missing cells are explicitly unverified rather than invented. No predictive model has been built.
